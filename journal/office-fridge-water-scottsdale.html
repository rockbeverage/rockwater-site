What to Stock in a Scottsdale Office Fridge (and Why Cans)

Scottsdale, Arizona · October 19, 2026 · By Brett Yanofsky

A simple guide for Scottsdale offices: what to keep in the fridge, why canned spring water beats plastic jugs, and how monthly delivery takes it off your list.

Every office in Scottsdale has the same fridge problem. Somebody stocks it once, it looks great for a week, and then it turns into a shelf of half-empty plastic bottles and a mystery salad. The fix is not complicated. It just takes a little intention about what goes in and a way to keep it full without anyone thinking about it.

Start with water, not soda

Water is the drink people reach for most at work, especially here. Arizona is hot and dry for a good part of the year, and people coming in from a lunch walk on Scottsdale Road or a site visit in the afternoon sun want something cold. If the fridge is mostly soda and energy drinks, water ends up being the plastic bottle someone brought from home.

Make water the default. Put it front and center, at eye level, so it is the first thing people see when the door opens.

Why canned water fits an office

Plastic jugs and cases of bottles are the usual answer. They also take up space, leave a recycling bin full of plastic, and rarely feel like something a team is proud to hand a client.

A 12oz aluminum can solves most of that:

  • It stacks. Cans fit neatly on a fridge shelf or in a drawer cooler, so you can see how many are left at a glance.
  • It chills fast and stays cold. Aluminum gets cold quickly, which matters on a busy afternoon.
  • It recycles. Aluminum is infinitely recyclable. No plastic bottles piling up by the sink.
  • It looks good on the conference table. Setting a cold can in front of a client says you thought about the details.

A simple office fridge list

Keep it short. A fridge with fewer, better things stays organized longer.

  • Still water as the main drink, stocked so it never runs out.
  • One or two coffee options for the afternoon, like cold brew.
  • A small snack shelf with things people actually eat, such as fruit, yogurt or protein bars.
  • A clear rule about labeled lunches and a weekly clean-out day.
