Patio Season in Scottsdale: Where to Grab a Cold Drink

Scottsdale, Arizona · November 4, 2026 · By Brett Yanofsky

A local guide to patio season in Scottsdale: the neighborhoods worth a slow afternoon outside, how to plan a patio day, and what to keep cold at home.

Patio season in Scottsdale is the stretch from fall through spring when the heat finally lets go and the whole city moves outside. Lunch happens at a table in the sun. Dinner runs late under string lights. A good place to grab a cold drink is anywhere there is shade, a good view and nowhere you need to be. Old Town Scottsdale is the easy answer, but the season reaches every corner of the Valley, from Paradise Valley to Fountain Hills, and it works just as well on your own back patio.

Why patio season in Scottsdale feels different

Arizona summers are hot and dry. For months, outside time gets planned around early mornings and late nights. Then fall arrives and the rules change. Doors open. Misters switch off. People who spent the summer moving from car to air conditioning to car start lingering again.

That shift is what makes patio season here a real season and not just good weather. It is the part of the year Scottsdale is built for: wide sidewalks, courtyards, fire pits, mountain views and long afternoons that turn into evenings without anyone deciding they should.

Where to spend a patio afternoon

You do not need a list of specific spots. You need the right part of town and a little time. A few places we keep coming back to:

  • Old Town Scottsdale. The center of it all. Walkable blocks, shaded courtyards, galleries and plenty of outdoor seating. On Scottsdale ArtWalk nights, the streets fill and the patios stay busy well after dark.
  • North Scottsdale near Pinnacle Peak. Quieter, with open desert around you and a clear look at the mountains. Good for a slow lunch after a morning on the trail.
  • Near the McDowell Sonoran Preserve. Finish a hike at Gateway or Tom's Thumb, then find a table nearby and sit for a while. Rest feels better when you earned it.
  • Paradise Valley and Arcadia. Camelback Mountain in the background makes almost any patio feel like a postcard.
  • Fountain Hills. A short drive east, slower pace, wide open views. Worth it on a clear afternoon.

Each one has its own feel. The common thread is the same: sun, shade, and a cold drink on the table.

How to plan a good patio day

Patio season is relaxed by design, but a few small habits make it better.

  • Pick your side of the sun. Fall and spring afternoons can still be warm. Ask for shade early in the day and sun later as it cools.
  • Bring a layer. Desert evenings drop off fast once the sun goes down.
  • Carry water. Dry air pulls moisture out quietly, even when it does not feel hot. Drinking regularly is standard advice for anyone spending a few hours outside in Arizona. For heat guidance, the National Weather Service in Phoenix is a good place to check.
  • Park once. In Old Town especially, the simplest plan is to leave the car and walk.

We wrote more about everyday habits for the dry months in staying hydrated in Arizona heat. Most of it applies to a long patio afternoon too.
