Golf Course Beverages in Scottsdale: Why Aluminum Fits the Cart

Scottsdale, Arizona · November 16, 2026 · By Brett Yanofsky

Why Scottsdale golf clubs add canned spring water to the beverage cart and halfway house, with simple case pricing and free monthly delivery from a local brand.

If you run food and beverage at a club and are rethinking golf course beverages in Scottsdale, here is the short version: put a canned spring water on the cart and in the halfway house, keep it cold, and set it on a monthly delivery. Aluminum handles a bumpy cart path, a sunny tee box and a packed ice bin better than most packaging, and it fits the way members already play in Scottsdale, Paradise Valley and Fountain Hills.

Why the beverage cart is a different kind of shelf

A cooler in a clubhouse sits still. A beverage cart does not. It rolls over cart paths all morning, sits in direct sun between holes and gets restocked from an ice chest fast when a group waves it down. Whatever you put on it has to survive all of that and still look good when it gets handed over.

Arizona is hot and dry, and golfers are outside for hours. Carrying water and drinking regularly is standard advice for anyone outdoors here, so water is usually the first thing a group asks for on the back nine. For heat guidance, point members and caddies to the National Weather Service Phoenix.

Brett Yanofsky, who founded ROCK, worked as a golf attendant at a Scottsdale club while he was at Arizona State. He has seen the cart from the inside, which is a big part of why ROCK is built the way it is.

Why aluminum fits the course

Every ROCK comes in a 12oz aluminum can. That choice matters more on a golf course than almost anywhere else.

  • It chills fast. A can buried in ice gets cold quickly, so a restock between turns is ready by the next group.
  • It travels well. Cans stack tight in a cart bin and do not roll around or crush the way thin plastic does.
  • It is the right size. 12oz is easy to finish between a tee shot and a putt, so fewer half-full drinks get left in cup holders.
  • It recycles. Aluminum is infinitely recyclable, and a single bin by the turn or the bag drop handles it. We will never use plastic.

We wrote more about that decision in why we chose aluminum. For the member side of the same idea, see our guide to a golf weekend in Scottsdale.

What members see when they grab one

Members notice what a club chooses to serve. A clean can with a short label reads as intentional, and it matches a well-kept clubhouse better than a generic case from a warehouse store.

The label is simple enough for any cart attendant to explain in one line. ROCK is natural spring water with minerals and electrolytes, 0 sugar, 0 calories and nothing artificial. Sweat carries out water and some electrolytes, so people who are active or outdoors in the heat often pay closer attention to both. If anyone wants more, our plain-language piece on electrolyte water keeps it simple.
