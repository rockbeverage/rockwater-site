How to Stay Hydrated in Arizona Heat: Simple Scottsdale Habits

Scottsdale, Arizona · October 27, 2026 · By Brett Yanofsky

Simple, everyday habits to stay hydrated in Arizona heat, from early hikes in Scottsdale to long afternoons around the Valley, without the hype.

How do you stay hydrated in Arizona heat? Keep it simple. Carry water every time you leave the house, drink regularly instead of waiting until you are thirsty, and plan your day around the hottest hours. That is the standard advice for hikers and anyone outdoors, and it works just as well for a regular day in Scottsdale. Arizona summers are hot and dry, so the habits matter more here than almost anywhere. Below are the ones we come back to, from the morning trail to the afternoon carpool line.

Why Arizona heat asks more of you

Dry heat is easy to underestimate. You sweat, and in the desert air it can feel like you barely sweat at all. But sweat still carries out water and some electrolytes, which is why people who are active or outdoors in the heat often pay closer attention to both. Electrolytes such as sodium, potassium and magnesium are minerals the body uses to help maintain fluid balance. If you want the plain version of how that works, our earlier piece on electrolyte water in Phoenix covers it, and the electrolytes page goes a little deeper.

None of this is medical advice. For real heat safety guidance, go to the people whose job it is. The CDC heat and health pages and the National Weather Service in Phoenix are the places to check, especially on days with heat warnings.

Simple habits to stay hydrated in Arizona heat

The habits that hold up are the boring ones. They do not depend on willpower. They depend on having water within reach.

  • Start before you go out. Drink with breakfast, not just once you are on the trail or on the course.
  • Carry more than you think you need. A hike up Camelback Mountain or Pinnacle Peak always takes longer than the map says.
  • Drink on a rhythm. A few sips at every rest stop, every hole, every break. Regular beats a lot all at once.
  • Keep water in the car. Errands in Scottsdale, Tempe or Mesa turn into long afternoons fast.
  • Move outdoor plans early. Dawn hikes and early tee times are a local habit for a reason.
  • Pay attention to the official forecast. On heat warning days, follow the guidance from Maricopa County and the National Weather Service.

A day in Scottsdale, hour by hour

Here is what this looks like on a normal summer day in the Valley.

Morning

The cooler part of the day belongs to the trail. Tom's Thumb in the McDowell Sonoran Preserve, the Gateway loop, or a quick climb on Piestewa Peak in Phoenix. Bring water for the way up and the way down, and drink before you start. Leave a cold can in a cooler in the car for after.

Midday

Most of us are inside by now, at a desk or running between meetings. This is when hydration quietly slips. Keep something on your desk or in the office fridge so it is easy. If your team is in Old Town Scottsdale or out near Gilbert and Chandler, a stocked fridge does more than a reminder on your calendar.

Afternoon and evening

School pickup, practice on the sidelines, a walk once the sun drops. Then patio season takes over for most of the year, from Old Town to Arcadia. Same rule all day: have water with you, and sip it on a rhythm.
