Pool Day in Scottsdale: The Weekend Cooler List

Scottsdale, Arizona · November 6, 2026 · By Brett Yanofsky

Planning a pool day in Scottsdale? Here is the simple cooler list we use: cold water, easy food, shade, sun cover and a plan for the empties at the end of the day.

What do you pack for a pool day in Scottsdale? A cooler full of ice and cold drinks, mostly water, plus easy food, real shade and sun cover. That is most of it. A weekend at the pool is one of the simplest plans in Scottsdale, whether it is a backyard in North Scottsdale, a friend's place in Paradise Valley or a community pool in Old Town. The heat and the dry air are what make it different from a pool day anywhere else, so the cooler does more work than people expect. Here is the list we use.

The pool day cooler list

Start with the cooler itself. A hard-sided one keeps ice longer in the afternoon sun, and a lid that latches keeps the heat out between trips. Pack it heavy on ice and light on everything else.

  • Water, first and most. More than the group thinks it needs. On a long afternoon in the sun, the water runs out before anything else.
  • Cans over glass. Glass by the pool is a hazard nobody wants to clean up. Cans chill fast and stack tight in the ice.
  • Easy food. Cut fruit, sandwiches, chips, something salty. Nothing that needs a grill or a knife on the deck.
  • A bag of extra ice. Keep it in a second cooler or the freezer and top off around midday.
  • A bin for the empties. Rinse and recycle at the end of the day instead of hunting for them under the chairs.

Shade, sun cover and the rest of the bag

The cooler is half the plan. The other half is keeping everyone comfortable from late morning into the afternoon. Set up an umbrella or find the covered patio before you claim a chair. Bring sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses, and reapply after every swim. Towels, a speaker turned low, a book and a charged phone round it out.

Even in fall and spring, the Scottsdale sun is strong by midday. Check the forecast from the National Weather Service in Phoenix before you plan the day, and look at the CDC heat and health pages for real heat safety information. We make water. We are not the ones to give medical advice.

Why water belongs at the top of the cooler

Arizona summers are hot and dry, and the air stays dry most of the year. In the pool it is easy to forget you are sweating at all. Sweat carries out water and some electrolytes, so people who are active or outdoors in the heat often pay closer attention to both. Electrolytes such as sodium, potassium and magnesium are minerals the body uses to help maintain fluid balance. Our piece on electrolyte water in Phoenix explains it in plain terms, and simple habits for Arizona heat covers the everyday side.

The habit that works at the pool is simple. Take a drink every time you get out of the water. Keep a cold one in the shade next to your chair. When the cooler gets opened, someone grabs a water, not just whatever is on top.

Questions

What should I bring to a pool day in Scottsdale?

Bring a hard-sided cooler with plenty of ice and water, easy food, sunscreen, a hat, sunglasses and towels. Plan for shade with an umbrella or covered patio, and skip glass on the pool deck.

How much water should I pack for a pool day in Arizona?

Pack more water than the group thinks it needs, since the dry air and sun make it easy to sweat without noticing. On hot days, follow guidance from the National Weather Service Phoenix and the CDC heat and health pages.

Why are cans better than glass at the pool?

Cans do not shatter on a pool deck, they chill quickly in ice and they pack tightly in a cooler. Aluminum is also infinitely recyclable.
