South Mountain Hike in Phoenix: A Simple Sunrise Trail Day

Phoenix, Arizona · November 20, 2026 · By Brett Yanofsky

Planning a South Mountain hike in Phoenix? Start at sunrise, pick a trail that fits the morning, carry plenty of water and keep a cold can waiting in the car.

Thinking about a South Mountain hike in Phoenix this weekend? Go at sunrise, pick a trail that fits the time you have, carry more water than you think you need, and leave a cold drink in the car for the end. South Mountain Park and Preserve sits on the south edge of Phoenix, a short drive from Downtown Phoenix, Tempe and Chandler. It has trails for an easy walk and trails for a long, hard morning. Here is the simple plan we use.

Why a South Mountain hike at sunrise

Sunrise is our favorite hour on this mountain. The light comes in low across the desert, the ridges turn gold, and the city below is still quiet. It is also the cooler part of the day, and in Phoenix that window is short. A late start can turn a relaxed walk into a hot, slow one, even in fall and spring.

South Mountain is wide open. There is very little shade on most trails, and the rock holds heat once the sun gets high. Starting early gives you the light, the views and the easier conditions, all at once.

Pick a trail that fits the morning

The preserve is big, with trailheads on more than one side of the mountain. That is the nice thing about it. You can make the day as short or as long as you want.

  • An easy morning. Choose a flatter loop near a trailhead. Good for a walk with a friend, a dog or a first desert hike.
  • A real workout. Choose a trail that climbs toward the ridgeline. Expect rocky footing and a steady climb.
  • A long day. Link trails together along the ridge. Plan your water around the full distance, not just the first half.

Whatever you pick, look at a trail map before you leave, tell someone where you are going, and turn around early if the heat builds faster than you expected. The City of Phoenix posts park and trail updates.

What to pack for a South Mountain hike

The list is close to the one in our Camelback hike guide, with a little more room for distance.

  • Water. Carrying water and drinking regularly is standard advice for anyone hiking in Arizona. On longer South Mountain routes, bring enough for the whole loop.
  • Shoes with grip. Loose rock and gravel are common on the climbs.
  • Sun cover. A hat, sunglasses and sunscreen, even at first light.
  • A small pack. Something that carries water comfortably and keeps your hands free.
  • A charged phone. For a map, photos from the lookouts and a call if you need one.
  • A headlamp. If you start before the sun is fully up, the first part of the trail can still be dark.

Before you go, check the forecast from the National Weather Service in Phoenix. For real heat safety information, the CDC heat and health pages are the place to start. We make water. We are not the ones to give medical advice.

Water on the trail, kept simple

Desert air is dry, and on a long hike you lose more water than it feels like. Sweat carries out water and some electrolytes, so people who are active or outdoors in the heat often pay closer attention to both. Electrolytes such as sodium, potassium and magnesium are minerals the body uses to help maintain fluid balance. Our piece on electrolyte water in Phoenix covers the basics in plain words.

The habit that works is a rhythm. Drink before you leave the car. Take a few sips at each lookout or flat stretch. Drink again before you turn back, because the return often takes longer than people plan for.

Questions

What time should you hike South Mountain in Phoenix?

Go at sunrise, because the light is beautiful and it is the cooler part of the day. Most trails have little shade, so start early and check the National Weather Service Phoenix forecast before you go.

Is South Mountain a good hike for beginners?

Yes, South Mountain Park and Preserve has easier loops near the trailheads as well as harder climbs toward the ridge. Beginners can pick a flatter trail and turn around whenever they want.

What should I bring on a South Mountain hike?

Bring plenty of water for the full route, shoes with good grip, a hat and sunscreen, a charged phone and a small pack. If you start before sunrise, a headlamp helps on the first stretch of trail.
